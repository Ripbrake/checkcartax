A complete guide to car tax

Car tax, officially Vehicle Excise Duty and also called road tax, is older than the British motor industry. Here's how it started and how it works today.

UK

A brief history

A duty on mechanically propelled vehicles goes back to the late 19th century, before cars were a common sight in Britain. In the early 20th century the idea took hold that motorists should pay for the roads, which is where the name "Road Fund Licence" came from. That link to road spending was eventually dropped and the money now goes into general taxation, but the nickname "road tax" has stuck.

For decades, cars paid a rate based on engine size. From 1 March 2001 new cars moved to a system based on CO2 emissions, and from 1 April 2017 a new structure brought in first-year rates, a flat standard rate and a supplement for expensive cars. The paper tax disc was abolished in 2014.

Exempt vehicles

Some vehicles don't pay, but must still be taxed (at £0). These include vehicles used by a disabled person (on one vehicle at a time), historic vehicles built before 1 January 1986, mobility vehicles, steam vehicles and some agricultural vehicles. From 1 April 2025, electric cars, vans and motorcycles lost their exemption and now pay vehicle tax.

Paying, refunds and SORN

If you pay by Direct Debit, monthly and 6-monthly payments carry a 5% surcharge; paying yearly has no surcharge. DVLA takes payments on the first working day of the month, and the Direct Debit renews automatically when the tax runs out.

If you sell, scrap or declare a vehicle off the road (SORN), DVLA cancels the tax and refunds any full months left. You can't use a SORN vehicle on the road until you tax it again.

Source: GOV.UK vehicle tax rate tables. Rates change each April, so always confirm on GOV.UK before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Is road tax the same as car tax?

Yes. Road tax, car tax, vehicle tax and road fund licence all refer to Vehicle Excise Duty (VED).

Which vehicles are exempt from car tax?

Examples include vehicles used by a disabled person, historic vehicles built before 1 January 1986, mobility vehicles and steam vehicles. You must still tax an exempt vehicle.

Do I get a refund if I sell my car?

Yes. When you tell DVLA you've sold the car, you'll get a refund for any full months of tax left.
