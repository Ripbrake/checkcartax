Why check a vehicle's tax?

Paper tax discs were scrapped in 2014, so you can no longer tell from the windscreen whether a car is taxed. DVLA holds the record against the registration number instead, which means a quick number plate check is the only reliable way to confirm a vehicle is taxed, untaxed or declared off the road (SORN).

  • Your own car: confirm the date your tax runs out so you never drive untaxed by accident.
  • Buying used: vehicle tax does not pass to the new keeper. You must tax a car before you drive it away, so check its status and expected rate before you buy.
  • After a change: if you have just taxed a vehicle or made a SORN, GOV.UK says records can take up to 2 working days to update.

How car tax is worked out

What you pay depends mainly on when the vehicle was first registered:

  • Before 1 March 2001: based on engine size (£230 or £375 a year).
  • 1 March 2001 to 31 March 2017: based on CO2 emissions, in bands A to M (£20 to £790 a year).
  • From 1 April 2017: a first-year rate based on CO2, then a flat standard rate of £200 a year, plus £440 a year for 5 years on cars with a high list price.

Since 1 April 2025, electric cars pay vehicle tax too. Read how much is my car tax for the full tables, or our complete guide to car tax.

Frequently asked questions

Is checking a car's tax status free?

Yes. Checking whether a vehicle is taxed only needs its registration number, and you can check any vehicle, not just your own.

Do I still need a tax disc?

No. Paper tax discs were abolished in 2014. DVLA keeps the tax record electronically against the number plate.

Do electric cars pay car tax?

Yes. From 1 April 2025 electric cars, vans and motorcycles have to pay vehicle tax.

Does tax transfer when I buy a used car?

No. Vehicle tax does not transfer to a new keeper, so you must tax the car yourself before driving it.
